01 — Ground rules

Start with the part that’s in your favour.

If a page opens by telling you vaping is as bad as smoking, close it. That isn’t true, and you already know it.

Measured in actual people: someone who only vapes carries 98% less NNAL — a tobacco-carcinogen marker — than someone who smokes. Cochrane rates it high certainty that vapes beat patches for quitting cigarettes.

Here is the turn. Every one of those numbers compares vaping to smoking. None compares vaping to nothing. On that second question, the same UK review is not ambiguous:

“…discourage people who have never smoked from taking up vaping.” Office for Health Improvement and Disparities, Nicotine vaping in England evidence review

Two different questions, two different answers. Evidence that vaping helps smokers quit is not evidence that vaping is good for you.

Vaper vs smoker, by toxicant marker

    Real, and large. Also not the comparison that applies to you if you never smoked. — OHID evidence review

    02 — Composition

    It is not water vapour. There is no water in it.

    Solvent, heated until it aerosolises — carrying nicotine, flavour chemicals, aldehydes from cooking the solvent, and metal off the coil.

    The last two cards are there deliberately. A page that only lists the alarming findings is a page you should stop trusting around card three.

    68.4 Median nickel in the aerosol of 56 devices taken from daily users, in µg/kg — against 2.03 µg/kg in the liquid those same devices came from. The difference is the coil. Olmedo et al., Environmental Health Perspectives

    Why it never feels expensive

    Between 2020 and 2022 a US disposable went from $8.49 to $14.07 — while the liquid inside fell 69%, from $7.96 to $2.45 a millilitre. You are getting an excellent deal on a larger quantity of the thing you would rather use less of.

    69% Fall in the price per millilitre of e-liquid in US disposables, 2020–2022, while the sticker price rose 66%. Tobacco Control, PMID 37536928
